SaaS Sender gives you a Gmail-powered, automated outreach engine that sends your CV directly to recruiters’ inboxes. This guide explains how to write templates that get responses — built specifically around how this platform works.

How SaaS Sender Sends Your Emails

Each email is dispatched through your own Gmail API credentials, meaning it arrives from your personal Gmail address — not a shared sender pool. Your CV (PDF) is automatically attached to every email. The platform queues up to 240 recruiter contacts per day and randomly rotates between your active templates (A, B, C) to avoid spam pattern detection.

Understanding Template Rotation (A / B / C)

In Settings → Email Templates, you configure up to three distinct templates. When a campaign is active, the platform selects one at random for each email sent. This is your primary anti-spam lever — no two consecutive emails are identical. Use this to your strategic advantage:

Template A  —  Direct

Lead with the role you’re applying for. Brief, formal, factual. Ideal for corporate or banking recruiters. State your years of experience and attach your CV in the first two lines.

Template B  —  Value-Led

Open with your most measurable achievement or skill. This performs better with tech and startup recruiters. Avoid job-title-first openers; focus on what you’ve delivered.

Template C  —  Curiosity

Ask a short, relevant question as the subject line. Keep the body to 2 sentences. Works best for creative or consulting roles where engagement matters more than credentials.

Using Spintax in Your Templates

The platform’s template engine supports spintax — a syntax that picks a random phrase variant each time an email is sent. Combined with template rotation, this makes every single email unique. Use the free Spintax Tester to preview your randomization before launching.

{Application|Interest|Enquiry} for [Role] — [Your Name]

Recommended Email Structure (Platform-Optimised)

Because your CV is always auto-attached by the platform, your email body should be short — its only job is to earn a click-to-open, not to narrate your career history. Aim for under 80 words. Here is the structure that performs best:

Blueprint v2.0 — Platform Tuned
Subject:  {Application|Expression of Interest} for [Role] — [Your Name]
Line 1:  {Dear|Hi} [Recruiter Name],
Line 2:  I am {reaching out|writing} regarding the [Role] {position|opportunity} at [Company].
Line 3:  With [X] years in [Field], I {bring|offer} a strong background in [Key Skill].
Line 4:  My CV is attached — I would welcome a brief {call|introduction} at your convenience.
Best regards, [Your Name].

Subject Line Rules (Critical for Inbox Delivery)

The platform sends through the Gmail API, which means deliverability is high by default — but a poorly-written subject line can still trigger Gmail’s promotions or spam tab. Follow these rules when filling in the Subject Line field in Settings:

  • ✓ DO: Include your name and a specific role. {Application|Interest} for Finance Manager — John Smith
  • ✓ DO: Use spintax to vary the first word. Gmail flags repeated patterns from the same sender.
  • ✗ DON’T: Use ALL CAPS, exclamation marks, or words like “URGENT”, “FREE”, “OPPORTUNITY” — these trigger spam filters.
  • ✗ DON’T: Use the same subject across all three templates (A, B, C). Each should have a distinct subject format to maximise variation.
  • ⚠ NOTE: Subjects are applied at send time. Changing them in Settings takes effect only on the next campaign activation (a new snapshot is created when you press Start).

Your CV: The Platform Does the Heavy Lifting

Go to your Dashboard to upload your CV as a PDF. Once uploaded and a campaign is started, the platform attaches it to every outbound email automatically — you never need to reference the attachment in your body text manually. Keep your body copy short and let the CV do the talking.

Good body closing

“Please find my CV attached. I would welcome a brief call at your convenience.”

Redundant (avoid)

“I am attaching my resume, which you can find attached below as a PDF attachment…”

Daily Quota: The platform sends up to 240 emails per day by default, spread across your assigned recruiter pool. Do not attempt to rush placements by inflating your template count — quality and variation already handled by the engine. Consistency over days beats volume spikes that Google throttles.
