When you start a new email outreach campaign, your excitement is at an all-time high. You want to reach hundreds of recruiters or potential clients immediately. However, if you're using a relatively new account or haven't sent high volumes recently, jumping straight to 240 emails a day is the fastest way to get your account flagged, throttled, or permanently suspended by Gmail.

What is Email Warmup?

Email warmup is the process of gradually increasing the volume of emails sent from a specific account. This "signals" to automated spam filters (like Gmail's AI) that you are a legitimate human sender and not a bot or a malicious actor launching a massive spam blast.

By mimicking natural human behavior—starting small and scaling up—you build what is known as Sender Reputation. A strong reputation ensures your emails land in the Primary Inbox instead of the "Promotions" tab or, worse, the Spam folder.

"Sender reputation is like a credit score for your email address. You have to earn the trust of the providers before they allow you to handle high-volume outreach."

Why is the cap 240?

Even after the warmup phase, we cap outreach at 240 emails per day for private Gmail accounts. While Gmail allows higher technical limits, our data shows that exceeding 250 automated "personalized" messages in a 24-hour window dramatically increases the risk of manual review. We prioritize your account security over raw volume.

Best Practices During Warmup

  • Use Spintax: Use our {Hi|Hello} syntax to ensure no two emails are identical. Identical emails sent in batches are a major red flag.
  • Don't Stop/Start Constantly: Frequent pausing and resuming can look erratic to filters. Let the warmup run its course.
  • Quality Content: Ensure your templates provide value. High reporting/spam rates from recipients will override even the best warmup strategy.
